Google declares its Universal Commerce Protocol the standard for agentic commerce, with Target and Steve Madden live

On Alphabet's July 22 earnings call, Google's chief business officer said the company "established the open-source Universal Commerce Protocol, UCP, as the new standard for agentic commerce," naming Target and Steve Madden as merchants now live. Google also described Universal Cart, which lets shoppers add items from different retailers across Google services and buy in a single checkout. The framing and adoption pace are Google's own on-the-record claims, not independent findings.

Google is building the rails that decide which catalogs its shopping agents can see and transact, and a first fashion merchant is already live - UCP feed readiness becomes a near-term checklist item for a Shopify fashion brand.

NK stack signal: AI stylist / conversational shopping, Google Gemini AI

Michaels formally launches Ask Mike, a Gemini-built AI shopping assistant, and reports early usage data

Michaels and Google Cloud announced on July 21 that Ask Mike, a conversational shopping assistant built on Gemini Enterprise, is live on michaels.com and the retailer's apps after a quiet May launch - with a company-reported six-week concept-to-production build, nearly 75,000 conversations, and over 60% of interactions focused on product discovery. Michaels separately told Modern Retail that shoppers who engage with the assistant convert at more than double the rate of traditional search; that figure is company-reported, not independently verified.

This is the closest public benchmark for an AI assistant on a brand's own storefront: discovery-first usage and a fast build validate the exact pattern a fashion brand building an AI stylist is pursuing.

Source: Bloomreach newsroom Category: consumer_research Scan: Weekly Editorial Curation Date: 2026-07-22

NK stack signal: AI stylist / conversational shopping, Agent platforms / model providers

New consumer survey: shoppers now narrowly prefer AI tools over brand websites

In a Bloomreach-commissioned survey of 4,040 US and UK consumers (fielded May 11-21 by Propeller Insights, published July 22), 41.4% said they would choose to shop through an AI tool versus 38% directly on a brand's site. 75.4% reported having used AI tools such as Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini for shopping decisions, and 80.4% said those experiences met or exceeded expectations. Bloomreach sells personalization software, so the research supports its commercial narrative and should be read as vendor-commissioned.

If product discovery keeps shifting into AI assistants, a brand's own assistant and its visibility inside third-party agents both become traffic-defense strategies rather than experiments.

Source: Algolia newsroom Category: search_discovery Scan: Weekly Editorial Curation Date: 2026-07-21

NK stack signal: AI personalization / client memory

Algolia makes Dynamic Facets generally available, auto-prioritizing search filters by shopper context

Algolia announced on July 21 that Dynamic Facets is generally available to its customers: the feature surfaces and reorders the most relevant filters (such as size, color, or occasion) in real time based on the query and shopper behavior, replacing fixed facet lists. Algolia's vendor-reported early results cite 10-15% higher click-through on filters in its strongest-performing markets.

Facet quality is disproportionately important in fashion search, where size and fit filters drive conversion - adaptive facets are worth evaluating for any headless storefront's discovery layer.

Ulta Beauty runs its first major TikTok Shop campaign: an Ice Spice livestream fragrance debut

On July 22, Ulta Beauty hosted a TikTok Shop livestream with Ice Spice to debut her first fragrance ahead of a September Ulta-exclusive retail release, Glossy reported on July 24. The stream - Ulta's first major TikTok Shop campaign since joining the platform in March - drew a reported 178,000 views and 5.95 million impressions, with limited quantities sold exclusively during the broadcast.

A concrete playbook for exclusive-drop livestream commerce, where the discovery-to-checkout loop happens inside a social platform rather than on the brand's own site.
